# Face Shapes Research — A1 Foundation Sheet (DRAFT · pending owner visual approval)

**Project:** G-Art Journey · Research/reference prototype, not a published lesson  
**Sheet title:** **Five Base Face Shapes — Front · Three-Quarter · Profile**  
**Status:** Design specification locked for first prototype; artwork itself NOT yet generated or approved.  
**Source context:** Gemini variant library of Asian/European adult female faces (21–25).  
**Scope constraint:** Exactly **five** base shapes — Oval, Round, Heart, Square, Diamond. No sixth base shape in this workstream.

## 1. Why this sheet exists

Establish one visually legible graphite reference atlas *before* beauty/style variations and before photorealistic portraits. A learner should recognize the main outer contours, the widest facial region, jaw transitions and chin shape **without hairstyle, makeup or lighting doing the classification**.

Categories are useful **visual design heuristics**, not rigid biometric or ethnic classifications. Real faces can lie between categories; the examples here are deliberately clear teaching exemplars rather than claims that everyone fits perfectly.

**Do not** produce a G-Art Journey mini-series, new public guide, website upload, or Asian/European comparison in this phase. Those require separate decisions after the A1 prototype passes.

## 2. Master layout — 5 columns × 3 rows

- **Format:** Landscape, preferred 3:2 artboard, e.g. 3000 × 2000 px for final composition; not a mandatory native image-generation pixel size.
- **Rationale for landscape exception:** five horizontally compared face shapes are the primary teaching subject; portrait would shrink each face too far. Standard G-Art portrait rule continues for ordinary lessons.
- **15 studies exactly:** columns in fixed order **OVAL · ROUND · HEART · SQUARE · DIAMOND**.
- **Rows:** **FRONT (0°)** · **THREE-QUARTER (~45°)** · **PROFILE (90°)**.
- Header: roughly 8–10% height; bottom note/legend: roughly 5–8%; give the rest to the heads. Use 5 equal columns, clean gutters, unobtrusive horizontal guides. Avoid thick colored boxes.
- Every cell includes **one head**: same face/identity across all three rows of its column. Every column should represent a different but natural-looking adult woman. Do not repeat an identical face across columns.
- Front row is the **primary classification reference**. 3/4 demonstrates volume/jaw/cheek transition. True profile demonstrates forehead–nose–lips–chin depth and jaw angle; **profile alone cannot prove the frontal width relationships**, so do not exaggerate profile into a diamond/heart silhouette.
- All eyes at a coherent eye-level camera height; no up/down tilt, lens distortion or different crops. At row 2 all turn toward the same side by ~45°; row 3 all turn the same side by exactly 90°.
- Preserve perceived head scale across views and columns while allowing actual length/width differences to remain visible. Do not stretch a Round head to the same long face as Oval.
- Hair pulled back/short unobtrusive hairline; reveal entire upper forehead, temples, cheekbones, jaw corners and chin. Show neck lightly, no shoulders dominating. Avoid loose fringe, glasses, earrings and head accessories.

## 3. Face-shape structural lock

The following are *relative drawing cues*, not hard numerical thresholds. Compare **forehead width (F)**, **cheekbone width (C)**, **lower-jaw width (J)**, **face length (L)** and chin geometry using front view.

| Shape | Primary geometry that must read clearly | Do NOT let it drift into |
|---|---|---|
| **OVAL** | Moderately longer than wide; C roughly similar to/slightly wider than F; smooth continuous cheek-to-jaw taper; softly rounded, not pointed, chin; no strong jaw corners. | Round (too short/full), Heart (pointed chin), Diamond (C dominates narrow forehead), Square (strong corners). |
| **ROUND** | Noticeably shorter/wider *relative to Oval*; full cheeks, continuous convex cheek–jaw line; rounded short chin; no sharp jaw corner. | Oval (long narrow lower face), Heart (sudden V taper), Square (boxy jaw). |
| **HEART** | Upper face/forehead looks visibly broader than lower jaw; F at least comparable to C; upper-to-lower narrowing; small tapered chin without an extreme artificial V. | Diamond (forehead becomes noticeably narrower than C), Oval (soft equal taper), Round (lower face too full). |
| **SQUARE** | F, C, J relatively similar; noticeable mandibular corners and substantial lower jaw; chin broad/softly squared, not pointed; not exaggerated masculine. | Oval (soft narrowing jaw), Round (curvy corners), Diamond (midface dominates narrow jaw). |
| **DIAMOND** | **C clearly the widest**; temples/forehead distinctly narrower than cheekbones; lower jaw tapers inward; chin neat but not necessarily needle-sharp. | Heart (F becomes as wide/wider than C), Oval (uniformly curved taper), Square (wide boxy jaw). |

**Critical paired discrimination:**
1. **Oval ↔ Round:** length-to-width impression and cheek fullness; both keep soft jawlines.
2. **Heart ↔ Diamond:** F versus C is decisive; hairline/temples must be visible.
3. **Oval ↔ Square:** smooth taper versus visibly maintained lower-jaw width/angle.
4. **Oval ↔ Diamond:** continuous gradual outline versus pronounced cheekbone-width peak.

No geometric overstatement solely for legibility. Shapes must look like plausible, attractive adult faces, not rigid icons or caricatures.

## 4. Viewpoint / identity continuity

- **Front:** true near-symmetrical frontal face with gentle neutral expression; both jaw angles and temples visible. This is where shape is determined.
- **3/4:** turn one consistent head ~45° to viewer's left; keep recognizably same nose bridge, eyelid geometry, mouth, chin and hairline as its frontal version; far-eye and far-cheek naturally foreshorten. Show cheekbone and jaw transition.
- **90° profile:** true clean profile, one visible eye in profile, no accidental second eye, consistent nose/lips/chin and credible ear placement. Jaw hinge below/near ear should connect naturally to chin. No impossible facial width inference from side alone.
- **Within each column:** same age, expression, face identity, hairline and proportions in all three views.
- **Between columns:** naturally distinct subjects. Avoid same-face syndrome, but do not force stylistic/ethnic stereotypes to manufacture differences.

## 5. Styling and typography

- **Media:** fine graphite pencil/technical portrait line art; thin but visible varied line weight; optional very light construction marks, restrained contour hatching; clean paper texture. Clear silhouette takes priority over detail.
- **No:** full color, strong graphite fill, glamour makeup, contour cosmetics, heavy blush, studio lighting, photo skin, dramatic lashes, portrait retouching, illustrative hair volume, hair falling over face, exaggerated beauty filters.
- **Expressions:** neutral or barely perceptible relaxed mouth; no wide smile distorting cheeks/jaw.
- **G-Art Journey identity:** warm cream paper, editorial spacing, the canonical **purple logo at top-left**. Insert the actual repo logo asset during deterministic assembly; never ask generative drawing to recreate a logo. Pastel accents may appear only in small text/guide markers; the faces remain monochrome graphite.
- **Headline:** `FIVE BASE FACE SHAPES` (large but compact).
- **Subhead:** `Same shape. Three views.`
- **Column headings:** `OVAL`, `ROUND`, `HEART`, `SQUARE`, `DIAMOND`.
- **Row markers:** `FRONT`, `3/4 VIEW`, `PROFILE`.
- **Bottom note:** `Read shape from the front. Use other views to study structure.`
- Avoid claiming that a visual form is typical of Asian/European people. Regional facial-feature/style variations come only after the five shape exemplars pass.

## 6. Execution strategy

1. **Preferred first trial:** generate one complete 5×3, 15-head atlas with coherent graphite style, fixed columns, fixed three views and minimal/no generative text. Add verified labels and canonical logo by deterministic composition.
2. **Mandatory full-resolution QA** for all 15 individual heads, including shape, anatomy, labels and scale. Zooming into one cell must not expose deformed noses, double eye structures or inconsistent jaws.
3. **Fallback (not a change in approved final layout):** if the generator cannot keep 15 identities/views/shape cues coherent, draw **five triptychs** (one per shape, three views), QA them independently, and assemble the SAME 5×3 atlas programmatically without stretching/cropping.
4. **Last resort:** generate the 15 cells individually, then assemble; do not accept shape drift just to keep a single image generation call.
5. No prototype should replace prior approved Human Drawing assets. Save source/QA only; no public website publishing before owner approves visual quality and classification.

## 7. QA acceptance / rejection

**Acceptance gates (all required):**
- [ ] Exactly five labeled columns and three labeled rows; 15 distinct view cells in correct order.
- [ ] Every FRONT is identifiable from outline/landmarks with hair hidden, without reading its label.
- [ ] Paired comparisons Oval/Round, Heart/Diamond and Oval/Square survive a blind outline-only inspection.
- [ ] Same recognizable person across Front, 3/4, Profile in each column; each angle is anatomically coherent.
- [ ] Face shapes are not confused by hairstyles, shadows, makeup or perspective.
- [ ] Full forehead/cheekbones/jaw/chin visible; no cropped anatomy.
- [ ] All text legible and spelled correctly; authentic G-Art logo embedded or deterministically overlaid.
- [ ] 15 heads sufficiently large for desktop/iPad zoom; if the full atlas is too dense, use independently QA'd source triptychs and larger views rather than lowering art quality.

**Reject/rework only defective cells** if they show:
- Oval becoming long Oblong or V-line Heart;
- Round becoming Oval;
- Heart losing broad forehead / becoming Diamond;
- Square softened to Oval or Round;
- Diamond forehead too broad relative to cheekbones / becoming Heart;
- 3/4 or profile losing identity, distorted eye/ear/nose/jaw anatomy;
- styling hiding critical contour.

## 8. Approval path

**A1 Spec drafted → A1 graphite prototype → owner visual QA → if PASS: lock five base exemplars → only then create shape-specific beauty variations and Asian/European feature/style sets.**

The Gemini spreadsheet remains a **variant design reference**, not ground truth for shape labeling. Duplicate EUR_OVAL_01 and EUR_OVAL_02 entries in the Asian tab can be removed from the working spreadsheet because they also appear in the European tab; do not remove the European originals.

**Explicitly out of scope now:** website mini-series, photorealistic portraits, additional face-shape categories, personality/beauty rankings, claims about ethnic prevalence.
