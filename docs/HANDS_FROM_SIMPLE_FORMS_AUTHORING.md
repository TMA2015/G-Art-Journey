# Draw Hands from Simple Forms — production specification

Status: **authoring complete; artwork not yet approved/published**

This is the first Phase 2A production item. It exists to make the hand lesson reproducible across future chats and image-generation runs without relying on conversation memory.

## 1. Learning outcome

A beginner should understand that a hand is built from a few simple 3D masses before fingers and details are refined.

The poster must teach:

1. palm block
2. thumb base / thumb wedge
3. finger group direction
4. finger joints and length rhythm
5. clean final hand with believable anatomy

The final result is not a stylized manga hand lesson. It is a transferable hand-construction lesson that can later support realistic, manga, manhwa, manhua, cartoon and digital drawing.

## 2. Visual identity

- portrait infographic, roughly 3:4
- warm paper / sketchbook background
- graphite / pencil drawing only for the hand artwork
- G-Art pastel accents only for title, arrows, step numbers and tiny callouts
- airy composition with large drawings; do not crowd the poster
- concise English labels
- no decorative character portrait is required
- no copied anatomy diagram or artist composition

The hand drawings should look like a good drawing lesson, not a medical chart and not a crude wireframe.

## 3. Main cumulative sequence

Use **one consistent neutral hand pose** for the five-stage sequence. Recommended pose: relaxed open hand, palm slightly turned toward the viewer, fingers naturally separated.

The same hand orientation and proportions must be preserved through all five stages.

### Step 1 — Palm block

Show the palm as a slightly tapered box / mitten-like mass.

Teach:
- palm has width, height and depth
- wrist enters below the palm
- knuckle line is not perfectly straight

Label idea: **Start with the palm block**

### Step 2 — Add the thumb base

Add the thumb mound / wedge from the side of the palm.

Teach:
- thumb begins lower than the finger knuckles
- thumb projects at an angle
- thumb is not a fifth parallel finger

Label idea: **Add the thumb wedge**

### Step 3 — Group the fingers

Place the four fingers as simple tapered cylinders or grouped block forms.

Teach:
- middle finger is normally longest
- index and ring are similar but not identical
- little finger begins lower and is shorter
- fingers fan rather than forming four parallel sticks

Label idea: **Place the finger groups**

### Step 4 — Mark joints and gesture

Refine each finger into phalange sections and add the natural arc of knuckles.

Teach:
- visible bend points
- finger segments taper
- finger tips vary slightly in direction
- joints follow the pose rather than a ruler-straight pattern

Label idea: **Find the joints and flow**

### Step 5 — Clean final hand

Convert the construction into a clean graphite hand.

Teach:
- soften box/cylinder construction
- preserve joint landmarks
- show simple nail indications only where useful
- keep line weight light and readable
- do not hide structural mistakes with shading

Label idea: **Refine the final hand**

## 4. Supporting mini-studies

Add two or three small studies below or beside the main sequence. They are **variations**, not cumulative steps.

Recommended studies:

### A. Finger length rhythm
A simple front-view diagram showing the relative finger lengths and curved knuckle arc.

### B. Thumb direction
Two tiny examples: thumb relaxed outward vs. thumb folded across the palm.

### C. Simple bent hand
A compact side/three-quarter example showing that finger cylinders overlap in depth.

Keep all mini-studies large enough to inspect.

## 5. Mandatory anatomy checks

Before the image can be accepted:

- every fully visible hand has exactly **five digits**
- one clearly identifiable thumb per hand
- no extra, fused or duplicated finger
- finger order is anatomically plausible
- fingers attach to the palm in believable positions
- little finger is on the correct side
- thumb base originates from the side/lower palm, not the knuckle row
- joints bend in plausible directions
- palm/wrist connection is coherent
- no accidental hand flip between cumulative stages
- the same pose, camera angle and hand identity continue through Steps 1–5

Inspect every hand at full resolution, including mini-studies.

## 6. What to avoid

Reject the artwork if it contains any of these:

- six fingers or four fingers on a complete visible hand
- duplicated thumb
- four parallel equal-length fingers
- thumb attached too high like another finger
- random finger bends without joints
- palm changing size between steps
- left/right hand switching during the sequence
- finished rendering that hides construction instead of teaching it
- pastel-colored skin or decorative color shading
- tiny labels that cannot be read on mobile
- busy background or ornamental manga character that distracts from the hand lesson

## 7. Poster text

Recommended title:

**Draw Hands from Simple Forms**

Optional subtitle:

**Build the palm, thumb and fingers before adding detail.**

Five step labels:
1. Palm block
2. Thumb wedge
3. Finger groups
4. Joints & flow
5. Clean hand

Small bottom note:

**Tip: compare your drawing with your own hand from the same angle.**

## 8. Image-generation brief

Create an original educational graphite drawing infographic titled **Draw Hands from Simple Forms**.

Use a warm off-white paper background. The hand drawings are monochrome graphite only. Pastel rose/peach/mint/lilac may appear only in small step numbers, arrows and callout labels.

Show one relaxed open hand in exactly the same pose and camera angle across five cumulative stages:
1. tapered palm block with wrist
2. add a correctly placed thumb wedge/base
3. add four finger groups with believable relative lengths
4. refine finger joints, taper and natural knuckle arc
5. clean anatomical final hand

Every complete hand must show exactly five digits, including one correctly placed thumb. Do not change hand orientation between stages. No extra or fused fingers.

Add two or three small supporting studies for finger-length rhythm, thumb direction and a simple bent-hand overlap. Keep them clearly labeled as variations.

Style: polished beginner art-school sketchbook lesson, soft graphite line, subtle paper texture, generous whitespace, crisp readable English, elegant G-Art Journey editorial layout. No medical-chart look, no character portrait, no colored skin, no copied diagram, no photorealistic rendering.

3. Correct/re-generate if any hand fails.
4. Convert approved image to optimized WebP.
5. Add the new staged guide and skill-intent record.
6. Add asset dimensions/hash.
7. Run full repository checks.
8. Review desktop/tablet/mobile.
9. Publish only after all checks pass.
