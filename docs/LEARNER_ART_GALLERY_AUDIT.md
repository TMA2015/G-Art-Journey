# Learner Artwork Audit — Gallery & Curriculum Direction

_Last reviewed: 2026-09-30_

Status: **curation/audit only — no learner artwork uploaded to the repository**

Purpose:
- use an 18-piece learner artwork sample to evaluate what the current G-Art Journey curriculum supports well
- identify missing skills that matter to the learner's actual drawing interests
- prepare a future **My Art Journey / Student Gallery** without publishing private or third-party material prematurely

Privacy / rights rule:
- do not upload these learner images to the public repository until the owner explicitly approves publication
- confirm whether each work is original, fan art/reference study, or contains third-party photographic/background material
- public gallery candidates should favor original work or clearly permitted material
- preserve the student's original artwork; do not AI-redraw it for the gallery

## 1. High-level learner direction

The sample strongly favors:
- anime / chibi character art
- face and eye design
- hairstyle and silhouette design
- character expression
- outfit / costume choices
- flat-color digital illustration
- playful original-character-like visual identity

The strongest motivational path is therefore **fundamentals in service of character art**, not fundamentals taught as isolated academic exercises.

## 2. Skill signals from the 18-piece sample

### Strong / emerging strengths
- character ideation and visual identity
- expressive faces
- recognizable chibi silhouettes
- varied hairstyles
- willingness to try different palettes and character moods
- repeated experimentation with full-body, bust, close-up and two-character compositions
- interest in clothing, accessories and themed settings

### Highest-value growth areas
- stable head construction across front / 3/4 / tilt
- torso / pelvis / shoulder structure and body proportion
- hands and hand perspective
- believable arm anatomy through shoulder → elbow → forearm
- clothing fitted around a body rather than placed as flat symbols
- light / value structure beyond flat color
- character-to-background integration
- perspective and original environment drawing
- consistent line cleanup / line weight
- composition with more than one figure

## 4. Provisional first-gallery shortlist

Do **not** publish until originality/rights are confirmed.

A balanced first gallery would use 6–8 works rather than all 18. Provisional candidates:

- **02** — full-body character design
- **07** — loose/crayon-style work; useful as a genuine journey marker
- **08** — strong motif/palette identity
- **11** — chibi full-body pose
- **13** — distinct portrait identity
- **15** — varied character/skin design
- **16** — cohesive muted palette and braided-hair design
- optional **04 or 09** — for dynamic pose / expressive face variety

This shortlist intentionally shows **range and development**, not merely the most polished pieces.

## 5. Archive / special-treatment group

Keep available as private/archive studies unless rights are confirmed:
- works containing photographic backgrounds: **05, 10, 14**
- works with visible branded/reference/fan-art cues: **03, 06, 10, 17, 18**, plus any others the owner identifies as fan art
- any work based closely on another artist's composition/reference should be labeled as a study rather than original art

## 6. Curriculum implications

### Existing G-Art Journey material directly matches the learner's needs
- **Hands** → supports the ambitious hand/gesture attempts in 04 and 14
- **Eye Structure** → relevant across almost the entire set
- **Hair Masses** → directly aligned with the learner's repeated hairstyle experimentation
- **Figure Proportions** → important for 02, 11 and future full-body characters
- **Fabric from Tension & Gravity** → important for 02, 10, 11 and costume-heavy future work

### Highest-priority missing / future lessons
1. **Anime Head Construction: front → 3/4 → tilt**
2. **Torso + Pelvis for Character Artists**
3. **Gesture & Weight for Chibi / Anime Poses**
4. **Hands in Character Poses** — applied follow-up to the current hand lesson
5. **Character Light & Shadow** — three-value / cel-shading first, then softer rendering
6. **Character + Background Perspective**
7. **Two-Character Composition & Interaction**
8. **Line Weight & Clean Digital Inking**
9. **Color Palette & Focal Point**
10. **From Reference to Original Design** — how to study without copying the final design

## 7. Phase 2B implication

Do not automatically resume the previously proposed generic sphere lesson as the next public production task.

Before Phase 2B resumes, compare two options:
- expand the existing short Light & Shade note into a deeper fundamentals guide, **or**
- build a learner-aligned **Character Light & Shadow** lesson that teaches the same value logic on a simple female character/head form

The second option may connect more directly to the learner's actual art while retaining sound value fundamentals.
