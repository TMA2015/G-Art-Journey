## 2. Topic A — Chibi Characters

### Lesson 1 — Draw Cute Chibi Proportions
**Planned slug:** `chibi-proportions`

**Primary skill:** understand how intentional head-to-body ratios create a cute chibi silhouette without losing balance.

**Teaching sequence:**
1. Compare roughly 2-head, 2.5-head and 3-head chibi proportions.
2. Build the head, tiny torso and simple pelvis as large readable shapes.
3. Place short arms and legs with coherent shoulder, elbow, hip and knee direction.
4. Simplify hands and feet without turning them into broken anatomy.
5. Compare silhouettes and choose a proportion for an original character.

**Visual evidence:** at least three clear proportion examples and one finished original chibi character.

**Try it:** redraw one simple character at two different chibi ratios.

### Lesson 2 — Chibi Faces & Expressions
**Planned slug:** `chibi-faces-expressions`

**Primary skill:** create readable chibi emotions by coordinating brows, eyes, mouth and cheeks.

**Teaching sequence:**
1. Start from one simple chibi face.
2. Establish a clear eye style without overcrowding the face.
3. Compare happy, excited, shy, surprised, annoyed and sleepy expressions.
4. Change brows, lids and mouth together rather than moving one feature alone.
5. Keep the same character recognizable across the expression set.

**Visual evidence:** one character shown consistently across a compact expression sheet.

**Try it:** draw three emotions using the same face shape and hairstyle.

### Lesson 3 — Chibi Poses & Outfits
**Planned slug:** `chibi-poses-outfits`

**Primary skill:** combine simple chibi gesture, balance and clothing silhouettes without hiding the pose.

**Teaching sequence:**
1. Start with a short gesture line and simple body masses.
2. Compare four readable poses such as standing, sitting, small jump and playful lean.
3. Add simple outfit shapes that follow the body.
4. Use one small prop or accessory only when it helps the character idea.
5. Finish one original chibi with a clean silhouette.

**Visual evidence:** several small pose studies plus one finished character.

**Try it:** keep one chibi proportion and design two different outfits and poses.

## 3. Topic B — Fairy-Tale Princess

**Public name is `Fairy-Tale Princess`, not `Disney Princess`.**

The learning goal is the broad appeal of fairy-tale princess character design while keeping all characters original to G-Art Journey.

### Lesson 1 — Design a Fairy-Tale Princess
**Planned slug:** `fairy-tale-princess-design`

**Primary skill:** combine face, hair silhouette, dress silhouette and one original motif into a coherent princess character.

**Teaching sequence:**
1. Begin with a simple graceful head and upper-body construction.
2. Choose a distinct hair silhouette.
3. Choose a dress silhouette that supports the character mood.
4. Add one original motif such as stars, leaves, ribbons, pearls or geometric ornament.
5. Refine one finished original princess without copying a recognizable franchise character.

**Visual evidence:** clear silhouette alternatives and one finished original G-Art princess.

**Try it:** make two princess designs by changing only hair silhouette, dress shape and motif.

### Lesson 2 — Princess Hair, Dress & Royal Details
**Planned slug:** `princess-hair-dress-details`

**Primary skill:** simplify elegant hair, dress structure and decorative details into readable drawing groups.

**Teaching sequence:**
1. Group hair into large flowing masses before strands.
2. Build the dress from bodice, skirt and sleeve silhouettes.
3. Show a few believable folds that follow gravity and body movement.
4. Add restrained royal details: small crown/tiara, ribbon, flower, pearl or trim.
5. Remove unnecessary decoration so the character stays readable.

**Visual evidence:** hair variations, dress-shape studies, selected details and one finished example.

**Try it:** use the same base figure for two different hair-and-dress combinations.

### Lesson 3 — Graceful Princess Poses
**Planned slug:** `graceful-princess-poses`

**Primary skill:** create elegant gesture, hand placement and skirt movement while keeping balance plausible.

**Teaching sequence:**
1. Find the gesture and supporting leg first.
2. Compare several graceful poses: relaxed standing, small turn, gentle greeting and walking step.
3. Place arms and hands so they support the gesture rather than float beside the body.
4. Let the skirt and hair follow the movement.
5. Finish one pose with a clear silhouette and calm story mood.

**Visual evidence:** several gesture thumbnails and one refined full-body princess.

**Try it:** draw one princess pose twice with different arm and skirt movement.

## 4. Locked visual language

### Reference family
Use the current **Manhwa / Webtoon Character** posters as the closest visual reference for layout mood and character-first presentation.

**Do not use the Digital Art poster language as the visual template.**

### Required qualities
- portrait teaching poster; preserve a comfortable vertical composition
- bright warm-paper / off-white background
- light pastel accents only
- generous whitespace
- character illustration is the main visual focus
- thin separators, small labels and restrained callouts
- 3–5 major teaching zones rather than many boxed micro-panels
- friendly but controlled typography
- short English-first captions using common/simple words
- small, consistent G-Art Journey identity; branding must not dominate the teaching content
- examples large enough to inspect on the normal lesson page

### Avoid
- large saturated color blocks behind every step
- many strongly colored rounded rectangles competing at once
- oversized bubble/marker lettering that consumes a large share of the page
- body text enlarged until each card feels crowded
- dense technical-dashboard appearance
- excessive stickers, stars, arrows or decorative marks
- tiny anatomy hidden inside crowded panels
- copying the exact composition of existing Manga/Webtoon/Manhua posters

### Topic palette

**Chibi Characters**
- cream / warm white base
- blush pink
- peach
- pale mint
- pale sky blue
- small warm yellow accents
- low saturation overall

**Fairy-Tale Princess**
- cream / warm white base
- blush
- lilac
- powder blue
- pale champagne / soft gold accents
- optional pale mint
- elegant rather than glitter-heavy

## 5. Character continuity and variety

- Within one progressive step sequence, keep the same subject, pose/camera relationship and handedness unless the lesson explicitly compares alternatives.
- Across different posters, use clearly different original faces, hairstyles and outfits so the new material does not repeat existing Manga/Webtoon/Manhua characters.
- Chibi Lesson 2 should keep one character consistent across expressions.
- Fairy-Tale Princess should use **three distinct original princess designs across the three posters** while keeping a coherent G-Art visual family.
- Topic cohesion comes from palette, typography and teaching layout—not from cloning the same face.

## 6. Originality / franchise boundary

For the public website:
- do not use **Disney** in topic titles, poster titles, metadata or public explanatory copy
- do not use Disney character names
- do not copy recognizable costumes, hairstyles, crowns, props, color combinations or signature motifs from specific franchise characters
- do not combine several iconic cues that would make a new character obviously identifiable as a specific copyrighted princess
- broad fairy-tale ideas are fine; all final princesses must be original G-Art Journey designs

## 7. Mandatory human/character QA

All existing Illustration Standard rules remain active.

Before owner review, inspect every poster at full resolution for:
- coherent head/face construction
- natural, internally consistent expression
- plausible stylized shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles
- exactly five digits including one correctly placed thumb on every fully visible countable hand
- no duplicated/missing limbs
- no floating props or costume parts
- no accidental extra eyes/features
- no important hands/feet cropped when they matter to the lesson
- no unexplained character identity change between progressive panels
- clothing and hair following the intended pose
- readable text at normal lesson-page width on desktop and iPad

For chibi, stylization may shorten limbs and enlarge the head, but anatomy must remain intentional and coherent. If a hand is simplified into a non-countable mitten-like graphic shape, it must read deliberately; do not show ambiguous three- or four-finger teaching hands.

## 8. Readability gate

A poster is not approved merely because the full-resolution file looks attractive.

Pass only when:
- headings are easy to scan
- ordinary instructional text is comfortable to read on the standalone lesson page
- the main examples are large enough to understand without View large
- color accents guide attention rather than compete for it
- whitespace clearly separates teaching stages

**View large is optional inspection, not a requirement for understanding the lesson.**

## 9. Production / approval sequence

To minimize drift:

1. Produce **Chibi Lesson 1** as the visual pilot.
2. Owner reviews layout density, text size, palette, anatomy and overall character feel.
3. Lock any corrections before generating Chibi Lessons 2–3.
4. Complete and approve all 3 Chibi posters.
5. Produce **Fairy-Tale Princess Lesson 1** as the princess visual pilot.
6. Owner checks originality boundary, elegance, text density and anatomy.
7. Complete and approve Princess Lessons 2–3.
8. Only after a full topic has 3 approved posters: optimize WebPs, add topic/lesson metadata, tests, audit, build and controlled Pages release.

Do not mass-generate all six posters before the first poster of each topic is owner-approved.
