# Landscape Drawing — production plan

Status: **COMPLETE — all 5 Landscape lessons owner-approved and staged for website release**

## 1. Category role

Landscape teaches how to **construct and compose scenes**:
- depth
- simplification of natural forms
- water and reflections
- perspective in streets/buildings
- final composition

It should not become a general Pencil Art course. Graphite is the drawing medium, but the learning goal is the scene.

## 2. Approved five-lesson map

1. **Build Depth with Three Layers**
   - foreground / middle ground / background
   - size, overlap, contrast
   - goal: understand why a scene feels deep

2. **Trees, Rocks & Clouds as Big Shapes**
   - group nature into large masses
   - do not draw every leaf, pebble or cloud puff
   - goal: simplify nature before detail

3. **Water & Reflections**
   - horizontal flow, reflected shapes, distortion, value
   - goal: draw convincing water without copying a perfect mirror image

4. **Draw a Street That Feels Deep**
   - horizon + one-point perspective + building boxes
   - reuse the existing strong academic logic from `street-with-depth`
   - goal: make a street recede convincingly

5. **Compose a Complete Landscape**
   - focal point, big shapes, foreground framing, value grouping
   - goal: combine the previous ideas into one finished scene

## 3. Approved Landscape series identity

## 4. Density rule for Landscape lessons

Each lesson gets **one infographic only**.

Maximum structure:
- **4 main teaching zones**
- plus one small footer/practice strip

Do not:
- place multiple lessons on the same poster
- make 6–10 tiny sub-sheets
- create dense grids of tiny examples
- shrink details just to fit more content

If a lesson needs more examples, split the content conceptually and keep only the most useful examples on the poster.

## 6. Lesson 2 — Trees, Rocks & Clouds as Big Shapes

**Learning question:** How can I simplify nature before adding detail?

Locked layout — 4 zones:
1. **See the big mass**
   - tree crown / rock mass / cloud mass
   - simple silhouette only

2. **Break into 2–4 large shapes**
   - tree: crown + trunk groups
   - rock: major planes
   - cloud: large overlapping lobes

3. **Add directional texture**
   - a few branch/needle marks
   - rock cracks following planes
   - cloud shading following volume
   - no tiny leaf-by-leaf rendering

4. **Finished mini-scene**
   - one simple natural cluster combining tree + rocks + clouds
   - enough detail to feel finished, still clearly mass-based

Footer:
- Try it: simplify one photo into only 3–5 big masses

## 7. Lesson 3 — Water & Reflections

**Learning question:** How do reflections behave differently from the object above them?

Locked layout — 4 zones:
1. **Water surface direction**
   - calm horizontal strokes
   - flowing/curved strokes for moving water

2. **Reflect the big shape first**
   - simplified reflected silhouette
   - vertically related to the object, but not a perfect copy

3. **Break and soften the reflection**
   - horizontal interruptions
   - softer edges
   - less detail than the real object

4. **Finished lake / river study**
   - one readable water scene with controlled reflections

Footer:
- Try it: draw the same tree reflection in calm water and slightly moving water

## 8. Lesson 4 — Draw a Street That Feels Deep

**Learning question:** How does one-point perspective make a street recede?

Locked layout — 4 zones:
1. **Eye level + vanishing point**
   - one clean horizon
   - one vanishing point

2. **Build the street with boxes**
   - road edges
   - simple building blocks
   - no decorative windows yet

3. **Repeat smaller forms into depth**
   - doors, windows, lamps, paving
   - spacing compresses with distance

4. **Finished street study**
   - foreground darker/clearer
   - distance lighter/simpler
   - one quiet focal point

Footer:
- Try it: redraw the same street with a higher or lower eye level

## 9. Lesson 5 — Compose a Complete Landscape

**Learning question:** How do I decide what the viewer notices first?

Locked layout — 4 zones:
1. **Thumbnail choices**
   - only 3 small thumbnails maximum
   - compare focal placement and big shapes

2. **Choose the focal point**
   - strongest contrast/detail around one area
   - secondary areas quieter

3. **Frame with foreground + value groups**
   - foreground framing
   - 3–5 large value masses
   - avoid equal detail everywhere

4. **Finished landscape**
   - one complete graphite scene
   - clearly shows focal point, depth and grouped values

Footer:
- Try it: make 3 tiny thumbnails, choose one, then develop only that one
