# G-Art Journey — Human Drawing Quality Audit Round 2

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Status:** LOCKED REVIEW BASELINE  
**Scope:** 8 live Human Drawing posters  
**Goal:** judge teaching quality by whether a learner can actually follow and reproduce the drawing, not by visual attractiveness alone.

## Audit rubric

A poster passes only when it is strong on the following:

1. **One clear lesson purpose** — do not let one poster quietly become three lessons.
2. **Reproducible steps** — each stage should be drawable by a beginner.
3. **Visible construction** — big forms and guide lines before polish.
4. **Continuity** — cumulative stages keep the same subject / pose / camera unless the lesson explicitly teaches a change.
5. **Anatomy / geometry** — plausible structure; five fingers when fully visible; perspective rules when relevant.
6. **Visual hierarchy** — teaching drawings get most of the page; title and decoration stay secondary.
7. **Low cognitive load** — readable line art, adequate whitespace, limited competing text blocks.
8. **Young-learner suitability** — plain language and no rigid claims that one face/body formula is universal.
9. **Distinctness** — neighboring lessons should not repeat large sections of the same teaching sequence.

## Findings

### 1. Draw a Face in Five Steps
**Decision: REPLACE / REFOCUS**

Strength:
- the five-step construction itself is usable.

Problem:
- the live poster also includes a full Common Expressions section and a Common Angles section.
- those are already separate lessons.
- the poster therefore teaches much more than its title promises and reduces space for the actual face-construction sequence.

Replacement direction:
- one clear face construction only
- large line-art steps
- head shape → guides → feature placement → hair mass → clean final
- keep feature detail simple
- no expression or head-angle mini-course on the same poster
- final face substantially larger

### 2. Five Everyday Facial Expressions
**Decision: REPLACE / REFOCUS**

Strength:
- the five final expressions are readable.

Problem:
- roughly half the poster rebuilds a base face from scratch.
- this duplicates Face Basics instead of spending space on expression mechanics.

Replacement direction:
- assume the learner already has one base face
- keep one same character
- compare neutral / happy / surprised / sad / angry
- enlarge eyebrows, eyelids/eyes and mouth changes
- include small feature-detail diagrams where useful
- line art first; minimal accent color only

### 3. Turn a Head: Five Useful Views
**Decision: REPLACE — HIGHEST PEDAGOGICAL PRIORITY**

Strength:
- the lower five-view comparison is useful.

Problem:
- the main Step by Step row constructs a front-facing head through five stages rather than actually teaching a head turn.
- the true lesson objective appears mainly in the lower comparison section.

Replacement direction:
- five actual construction views: front, 3/4, side, looking up, looking down
- sphere + jaw + curved center line + eye line visible in each
- show how guides wrap the form
- keep hair/detail minimal
- no polished beauty rendering that hides the construction

### 4. Faces at Different Ages
**Decision: KEEP / COPY WATCH**

Strength:
- clear side-by-side comparison
- construction and final examples are easy to scan.

Watch:
- age examples are stylized and should remain illustrative choices, not universal anatomy rules.
- current guide copy already contains this qualification.

No artwork replacement now.

### 5. Understand Simple Figure Proportions
**Decision: REVISE LATER / SECOND PRIORITY BATCH**

Strength:
- useful head-unit concept and body landmarks.

Problem:
- one poster currently carries figure construction, proportion rules and pose/view examples.
- the core proportion lesson competes with additional content.

Future direction:
- simplify around one neutral full-body figure
- head units + shoulder / waist / hip / knee / ankle landmarks
- make the central proportion figure larger
- remove unrelated pose/view expansion from the same poster

### 6. Draw a Standing Figure Step by Step
**Decision: KEEP**

Strength:
- construction remains visible
- line of action → chest/hips → limbs → clothing → final is understandable
- pose variations add practice without breaking the main lesson.

It is usable, though not necessarily the new visual benchmark.

### 7. Five Relaxed Sitting Poses
**Decision: KEEP — STRONG**

Strength:
- construction row and finished row map clearly to each other
- support and bent-limb logic is readable
- five pose choices are useful reference vocabulary.

No replacement needed.

### 8. Body Shapes and Character Silhouettes
**Decision: KEEP / COPY WATCH**

Strength:
- underlying structure is visible
- multiple silhouettes are easy to compare.

Watch:
- avoid interpreting the illustrated age/build categories as fixed real-world body rules.
- current lesson copy already frames them as optional design variations.

No artwork replacement now.
